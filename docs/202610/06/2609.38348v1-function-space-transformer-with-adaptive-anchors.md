## 摘要
许多形式的数据，包括物理场、几何形状和视觉信号，本质上是由连续域上的函数所描述，但通常通过离散样本进行观测。在固定的均匀网格上表示这些函数，需要在解析局部变化与增加全域计算量之间进行权衡。神经算子通过学习函数之间的映射来解决这种不匹配，而潜在注意力架构则提供了对采样观测值的灵活处理。我们引入了函数空间 Transformer (FST)，这是一个通过空间自适应连续潜在表示从函数中学习的框架。FST 将特征存储在锚点上，这些锚点的位置由输入观测值预测得出，并通过函数空间交互递归地细化这些锚点特征。这使得该表示能够根据每个输入调整其空间组织，而不是继承观测网格的空间结构，同时支持空间解析输出和有限维输出。在基于 PDEBench Burgers 和 Darcy 流的偏微分方程 (PDE) 求解预测任务中，FST 的表现显著优于缺乏显式空间组织的 Perceiver IO 基准模型，并与傅里叶神经算子 (Fourier Neural Operator) 具有极强的竞争力。在 ImageNet-1K 上，FST 在参数量更少的情况下，实现了比视觉 Transformer (Vision Transformer) 基准更高的分类准确率。消融实验进一步支持了函数空间更新和递归细化的优势。总之，这些结果凸显了自适应连续表示在科学预测和视觉识别方面的潜力。

## Abstract
Many forms of data, including physical fields, geometric shapes, and visual signals, are naturally described by functions over continuous domains but are observed through discrete samples. Representing these functions on fixed uniform grids imposes a trade-off between resolving localized variation and increasing computation across the domain. Neural operators address this mismatch by learning mappings between functions, while latent-attention architectures provide flexible processing of sampled observations. We introduce the Function-Space Transformer (FST), a framework for learning from functions through a spatially adaptive continuous latent representation. FST stores features at anchors whose locations are predicted from the input observations and recursively refines these anchor features through function-space interactions. This allows the representation to adapt its spatial organization to each input rather than inherit that of the observation grid, while supporting both spatially resolved and finite-dimensional outputs. On PDE solution prediction using PDEBench Burgers and Darcy flow, FST substantially outperforms the Perceiver IO baseline, whose latent representation lacks explicit spatial organization, and is highly competitive with the Fourier Neural Operator. On ImageNet-1K, FST achieves higher classification accuracy than the Vision Transformer baseline, with fewer parameters across these comparisons. Ablations further support the benefits of function-space updates and recursive refinement. Together, these results highlight the potential of adaptive continuous representations for both scientific prediction and visual recognition.
